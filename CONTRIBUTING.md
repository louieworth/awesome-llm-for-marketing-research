# Contributing

Help improve Awesome LLMs for Marketing Research by suggesting relevant papers or correcting citations.

## Adding a paper

1. Choose the category and subsection by the paper's main contribution, using this order and structure: 1. Consumer Simulation; 2. Content Generation and Personalization; 3. Model and Algorithm Augmentation. Consumer Simulation has 1.1 Capabilities and Opportunities, 1.2 Validity and Limitations, and 1.3 Methods and Improvements. Place demonstrations and use cases in 1.1; behavioral, group, statistical, and causal validity studies and validity-centered reviews in 1.2; and papers whose central contribution is an explicit methodological innovation in 1.3. For 1.3, identify the new estimator, calibration objective, algorithm, probabilistic model, response-measurement procedure, or computational evaluation method and its supporting derivation or validation. Comparing models, prompts, interviews, data depth, or reasoning settings alone does not qualify. Supporting papers outside the strict method list remain in [the method supplement](docs/consumer-simulation-method-supplement.md); the [method review](docs/consumer-simulation-review.md) records their placement. Content Generation and Personalization has 2.1 Market Effects and Limitations for advertising, persuasion, and market-outcome evidence or boundary conditions, and 2.2 Methods and Improvements for personalized generation, feedback alignment, creative optimization, and information design. Model and Algorithm Augmentation has 3.1 Performance and Limitations for algorithm evaluations, reproducibility, and synthetic-training risks, and 3.2 Methods and Improvements for choice-design, pricing, demand, online-learning, recommendation, customer-text, and prediction or measurement methods. Classify computer-science papers by their contribution rather than their venue. Preserve the three, two, and two subsections. Include relevant LLM methods, datasets, and limitation research, plus the selected visual generative-AI marketing studies described below.
2. Verify the title, author order, venue or source identifier, date, and paper link against primary sources.
3. Add a reference as a Markdown unordered list item using the format below. Quote the title and place the paper link at the end with the label [link]. Use the verified journal or workshop name, or the arXiv/SSRN identifier; omit the venue when none is established.
4. Give each paper one canonical entry and update the category counts using unique papers. Dataset papers may also appear in 1.4, 2.3, or 3.3 while remaining in their original thematic subsection; link the repeated title to the canonical entry and count the paper once in its canonical category. Within every paper subsection, place published journal and conference papers first, ordered by the cited YYYY-MM date from oldest to newest, followed by unpublished papers in the same date order. Treat preprints, working papers, research records, and forthcoming papers without an online publication date as unpublished. Retain the existing relative order for equal dates and place entries without a verified month last within their publication group. Do not add publication-status headings or notes. Keep Selected Foundational Papers and supplementary paper lists ordered by cited date from oldest to newest. Use a unique, stable title-based paper anchor for internal links; keep existing anchors unchanged when adding papers. Selected Foundational Papers may repeat a citation and link its title to the canonical entry in the main list or a supplement.

```markdown
<a id="paper-descriptive-title-slug"></a>

- Author, First name, et al. "Full paper title." Venue or arXiv/SSRN identifier (YYYY-MM). [[link]](https://primary-paper-url)
```

Use YYYY-MM dates: the first online journal publication month when available; otherwise the conference/proceedings publication month, the listed preprint's first posting month, or the date on the author-hosted draft. For a forthcoming paper without a verified journal publication month, a verified first preprint posting month may be used for ordering when explicitly labeled as such. If no month can be verified, flag it for verification rather than inventing a date. Use author names and title from the linked version. Identify a workshop by its full name rather than its parent conference alone. Keep entries limited to citation details and an external paper link, except for the short data descriptions and data links in benchmark and dataset subsections.

## Adding a benchmark or dataset

1. Place benchmark and dataset resources under 1.4 Benchmarks and Datasets, 2.3 Benchmarks and Datasets, or 3.3 Benchmarks and Datasets according to their research use. These resource sections supplement the existing three, two, and two paper subsections. Count each cited source paper once; data descriptions and links do not add to the paper counts.
2. Link to the official data source or an actionable download location. Include data links only for verified releases.
3. In 1.4, 2.3, and 3.3, use the original dataset paper or the provider's recommended citation as the entry, followed by one short data description and a `[[data]](URL)` link. Check the source paper before treating a resource as a standalone dataset. Keep each dataset in one entry rather than separate training and test entries. Preserve the source paper's existing thematic entry when also listing its dataset. Do not add tables. Keep descriptions to the data type and essential scale, distinguishing human and synthetic data where relevant; omit research uses and methodological advice.
4. Use a unique `dataset-` anchor and keep existing paper anchors unchanged. Cross-link a resource used across categories rather than duplicating it. Verify the release or version against its official documentation; keep raw dataset files outside the repository.

## Corrections

Include a primary source supporting a corrected title, author list, venue, date, or link. Link to papers externally rather than adding their full text to the repository.

## Scope and evidence

Keep the README as a clean reference list. Support scope decisions and corrections with primary sources, and distinguish inspected full text from abstract-only evidence. A general generative-AI label or a text encoder alone does not establish substantive LLM use. Retain research on LLM limitations and mixed-model methods when the LLM component is verified. Flag unresolved cases for verification. Merge preprint and published versions of the same work; match effect claims to the inspected version.

Marketing relevance and inclusion are separate decisions. Recommendation, ratings, product preferences, pricing, customer text, and advertising establish relevant marketing applications when supported by the paper. Selected cross-disciplinary capability, validity, and method studies may remain without a direct marketing task. Record a background exception or unresolved scope in the audit; do not infer LLM use from a generative-AI label or infer marketing relevance from a venue or a prior inclusion decision. Preserve existing citations and stable anchors during category migrations.

Selected visual generative-AI studies of advertising effectiveness and marketing outcomes are included in Content Generation and Personalization, even when their creative-generation component uses diffusion models. Classify these studies by their marketing contribution, and state their model type accurately in supporting audits.
